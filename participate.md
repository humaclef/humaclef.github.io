## Who the lab is for

* Researchers in NLP, information retrieval and machine learning working with historical or noisy data.
* DH scholars in history, linguistics, visual arts and literary studies.
* Professionals in cultural heritage institutions (libraries, archives, museums).
* Developers and data scientists interested in diachronic and interpretative data settings.

## How to contribute

Because the first edition is a workshop rather than a campaign, participation does not involve submitting system runs. Contributions are expected in the form of position papers, dataset and resource descriptions, presentations of ongoing initiatives, and posters.

> **To complete:** this section describes intentions, not decisions. Confirm which contribution types are accepted, whether they are peer reviewed, the page limits, the template (CEUR-WS single-column is the usual choice for CLEF working notes), the submission system (EasyChair or similar), and whether accepted contributions appear in the CLEF working notes.
