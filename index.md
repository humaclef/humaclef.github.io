HUMANITAS is a lab at CLEF (Conference and Labs of the Evaluation Forum) devoted to the evaluation and benchmarking of computational methods that support research, access, analysis, enrichment and interpretation across Digital Humanities (DH) and Cultural Heritage (CH).

**Where and when:** CLEF 2027, Bucharest, Romania, 14–17 September 2027 (half-day session, exact day and room to be announced).

## In short

Advances in language technologies, multimodal models and generative AI open new possibilities for humanities research, and at the same time they make the absence of adequate evaluation frameworks more visible. Historical and cultural collections are noisy, multilingual and complex, and the questions asked of them often involve interpretation and uncertainty rather than a single correct answer.

CLEF has a long tradition in this area (CHiC for retrieval, HIPE for named entity processing), but DH benchmarking efforts remain scattered across venues (PastReader and HISEMOTIONS at IberLEF, among others). The European research infrastructures CLARIN and DARIAH have built solid ecosystems of resources, services and communities, yet neither offers a dedicated space for systematic evaluation across datasets, tools and workflows.

HUMANITAS is intended as a sustainable umbrella initiative within CLEF: a place where evaluation methodologies and datasets accumulate over time, and a benchmarking layer that complements CLARIN and DARIAH.

## Goals

* Define evaluation challenges for DH workflows.
* Promote reusable, multilingual and historically grounded benchmark collections.
* Consolidate and advance evaluation methodologies adapted to historical and noisy data, and to research tasks that involve interpretation and uncertainty.
* Foster collaboration between DH scholars, technology researchers, CH institutions and research infrastructures.
* Establish a sustainable framework for shared evaluation campaigns within CLEF.

## First edition: a workshop, not yet a shared task

The first edition adopts a workshop format. Its purpose is community building, discussion of evaluation needs, assessment of available datasets and identification of priority challenges. Subsequent editions are expected to evolve towards campaign-style tracks with shared tasks and benchmark collections.

There is therefore no predefined shared task this year, and no system runs to submit. What the workshop produces is a roadmap: an agreed set of task scenarios, datasets and evaluation priorities that are suitable, impactful and sustainable for future CLEF campaigns.

## Who should come

Researchers in NLP, information retrieval and machine learning working with historical or noisy data; DH scholars in history, linguistics, visual arts and literary studies; professionals in libraries, archives and museums; developers and data scientists working in diachronic and interpretative settings.
